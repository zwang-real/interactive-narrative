Philadelphia · Yelp Open Dataset · 2022
How independent restaurants outlast chains in ratings but not in survival, and what the data reveals about who gets to stay.
By Susan Zhang & Zijing Wang
Introduction

On a side street in Philadelphia, a restaurant called Garces Trading Company appeared on Yelp in the winter of 2010. It had a four-star rating, nearly nine hundred reviews from people who called it a neighborhood favorite. By 2018, it was gone. No announcement, no explanation, just a listing that stopped updating.

This is not a story about one restaurant. It is a story about a pattern: one that repeats itself across thousands of independent businesses in cities like Philadelphia, hiding in plain sight inside the data.

Using Yelp's open dataset of over seven thousand food and restaurant businesses in Philadelphia, we trace the survival patterns of independent shops against chain restaurants, and find that the gap between them is structural, persistent, and largely invisible to the people living inside it.

Part 1 · The City's Pulse
On a street corner in Philadelphia, two restaurants open the same year. One is independent, the other a chain. From the outside, they look equally matched. But zoom out to the level of the whole city, and a different picture emerges. Not all neighborhoods offer the same starting conditions. Some cluster with thriving independents. Others are quietly dominated by chains.
Where They Stand — Rating & Survival Across the City
This map visualizes the geographic distribution of independent and chain restaurants across Philadelphia, encoding survival rate and average rating through color and shade. It sets the stage by showing that location alone creates unequal starting conditions.
More Reviews, Still Closing — Rating vs. Review Count
Each circle is a Philadelphia restaurant. The x-axis shows review count, the y-axis shows rating, and larger circles indicate more reviews. Dotted lines mark the mean review count and mean rating.
Part 2 · What Determines Survival
Independent restaurants close at higher rates despite better ratings. So what actually separates the ones that stay? The answer is not in the reviews. It is in the choices businesses make before a single customer walks through the door.
Delivery, Price, Location — Does Any of It Help?
Among Philadelphia restaurants, offering delivery is associated with a survival rate of 68.9%, compared to 33.6% for those without. Price tier, parking, and cuisine type each tell a different part of the story. Click through the variables on the left to see which choices move the needle.
Place Matters — Rent and Restaurant Survival
This map places neighborhood rent levels side by side with restaurant survival rates. Higher-rent, faster-growing areas tend to be harder places for independent restaurants to survive. Location is not just a coordinate; it is a recurring cost.
Part 3 · It Did Everything Right
The data has shown us the patterns. Now, carrying those patterns with us, we return to the restaurant from the beginning and check its report card, line by line.
The Report Card
Garces Trading Company offered delivery, held a four-star rating, drew nearly nine hundred reviews, had parking, and sat at a mid-range price point. By every measure we have seen, it stood on the side of the survivors. It should have survived. It did not.
